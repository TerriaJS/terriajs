# Colors

The color system is built around semantic intent, not visual abundance.
Instead of exposing large raw palettes, it defines a small, meaningful set of
color roles that cover most interface needs.

Most colors are derived from a few base values, which keeps contrast,
hierarchy and theming consistent, and the system easy to reason about and
change. Colors should communicate purpose and state first. Visual variation
comes from scale, emphasis and context.

Every color is a `--terria-ds-` custom property; the name under each color
is the rest of it. Each one switches with the theme: set `data-theme="dark"` or
`data-theme="light"` on any element, and everything inside it follows.

## Accent

The accent color represents the primary brand or product identity. It is used
to draw attention to key actions, highlights and moments of emphasis.

Use it intentionally and sparingly: overuse reduces its impact and can harm
visual hierarchy. Each role comes with a hover color, a foreground color for
text and icons on it, and a soft variant for subtler backgrounds.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Accent} />

## Default (neutrals)

Default colors form the neutral backbone of the system. They are used for most
non-emphasized UI elements.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Default} />

## Success

Success colors communicate positive outcomes, confirmations and completed
states. They are typically used in feedback components, status indicators and
validation states.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Success} />

## Warning

Warning colors indicate caution, risk, or actions that require attention but
are not destructive. They are commonly used for alerts, messages and
transitional states where the user should pause or review information.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Warning} />

## Danger

Danger colors represent destructive, irreversible or critical actions and
states. They should be immediately recognizable and used consistently for
errors, destructive buttons and critical alerts.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Danger} />

## Foreground

Foreground colors are used for primary content such as text and icons. They
are chosen for readability and adapt to the background and surface they sit
on. Never hard-code a text color in a component.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Foreground} />

## Background

Background colors define the base canvas of the interface. They establish the
overall contrast and mood while staying visually quiet.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Background} />

## Surface

Surface colors sit on top of backgrounds and are used for containers such as
cards, panels, modals and dropdowns. They create separation and hierarchy
through elevation, contrast and layering rather than strong color shifts.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Surface} />

## Form field

Form field colors are used for inputs, controls and interactive fields, across
their default, hover, focus and error states. Keeping them separate gives form
elements a visual language distinct from buttons and the rest of the UI.

<Story of={ColorsStories.FormField} />

## Separator

Separator colors are used for dividers, outlines and subtle boundaries. They
structure content and guide the eye without adding noise, so they stay low
contrast and unobtrusive. The status colors outline alerts and validation.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Separator} />

## Other

The default border, the backdrop behind modals, the overlay surface, the
selected segment of segmented controls, and keyboard focus.

<Story of={ColorsStories.Other} />

## Palette

The raw colors the roles above are built from, the same in both themes. Use
them to define new roles, not in components: a component that uses a
primitive directly won't follow the theme. Each one is
`--terria-ds-colors-<family>-<step>`, or `--terria-ds-colors-<name>` for the
ones without steps. Hover a color for its full name.
